## 2. Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg - Core Message

### Question 2

**How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P?**

B = MAP is BJ Fogg's Behavior Model.

- **B = Behavior** – The action or habit that we want to do.
- **M = Motivation** – How much we want to do the behavior.
- **A = Ability** – How easy or difficult the behavior is to do.
- **P = Prompt** – A reminder or trigger that tells us to do the behavior.

According to this model, a behavior happens when Motivation, Ability and Prompt come together at the same time.

### Question 3

**Why is it important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit?**

Shine or celebration is important because it creates a positive feeling immediately after completing a habit. When we feel successful after doing a behavior, our brain takes notice of that positive experience and we become more likely to repeat the behavior.

For example, if I complete my planned study session, I can simply tell myself, "Good job, I completed it." This small celebration makes the experience satisfying.

## 4. Book Summary of Atomic Habits

### Question 5

**What is the book's perspective about Identity?**

The book explains that lasting habit change is connected to our identity. Instead of only focusing on what we want to achieve, we should think about the type of person we want to become.

For example, instead of saying, "I want to learn programming," I can think, "I am becoming a software engineer." Then my daily actions should support that identity, such as practicing coding, solving problems and learning new concepts.

Every small action gives evidence about the person we are becoming. Therefore, changing our identity can help us build habits that last.

---

### Question 6

**Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do.**

The book says that we should make good habits easy by reducing the amount of effort or friction required to do them.

For example, if I want to study coding every day, I can keep my laptop and study material ready before I start. I can also break a large task into a very small task, such as solving only one programming problem.

Another important idea is the **Two-Minute Rule**. We can start with a very small version of the habit. Instead of saying, "I will study for two hours," I can start with "I will open my laptop and study for two minutes."

---

### Question 7

**Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do.**

To make a bad habit harder, we should increase the friction between ourselves and the habit.

For example, if I want to use my phone less while studying, I can keep my phone away from my study table.This makes it harder to pick up the phone whenever I feel distracted.

We can also make the cues for bad habits less visible.The main idea is to make the unwanted behavior more difficult and less convenient.

The opposite of the four laws can be used to break bad habits:

1. Make the cue invisible.
2. Make the habit unattractive.
3. Make the habit difficult.
4. Make the habit unsatisfying.

# 5. Reflection

## Question 8

**Pick one habit that you would like to do more of. What are the steps that you can take to make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and/or the response satisfying?**

One habit I would like to do more of is **daily coding practice**.

I can use the ideas from Tiny Habits and Atomic Habits in the following way:

1. **Make the cue obvious:**  
   I can keep my laptop and coding notes ready on my study table.

2. **Make the habit attractive:**  
   I can choose interesting JavaScript or DSA problems that I enjoy solving.

3. **Make it easy:**  
   Instead of deciding to study for two or three hours every day, I can start with just one coding problem or 15 minutes of practice.

4. **Use an existing habit as a trigger:**  
   After I finish my evening routine, I can start my coding practice.

5. **Make the response satisfying:**  
   After completing the problem, I can mark it as completed and appreciate the progress I made.

## Question 9

**Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of. What are the steps that you can take to make the cue invisible or the process unattractive or hard or the response unsatisfying?**

One habit I would like to reduce is **checking my phone unnecessarily while studying**.

I can use the four laws in reverse to reduce this habit:

1. **Make the cue invisible:**  
   I can keep my phone away from my study table and turn off unnecessary notifications.

2. **Make it unattractive:**  
   I can remind myself that checking the phone repeatedly breaks my concentration and makes studying take longer.

3. **Make it difficult:**  
   I can keep the phone in another room or inside my bag while studying.

4. **Make it unsatisfying:**  
   I can keep track of how many times I check my phone during a study session and try to reduce that number.
