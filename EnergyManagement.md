# Energy Management

## 1. Manage Energy, Not Time

### Question 1: What are the activities you do that make you relax - Calm quadrant?

* Meditation and deep breathing.
* Listening to music.
* Taking a walk outside.
* Watching something entertaining.
* Taking short breaks after completing a difficult task.

### Question 2: When do you find getting into the Stress quadrant?

* When I have multiple tasks to complete within a short time.
* When I am learning something difficult or unfamiliar.
* When I have an important deadline or assessment.
* When I overthink about my work.

### Question 3: How do you understand if you are in the Excitement quadrant?

* I feel energetic and motivated to work.
* I become curious and interested in learning new things.
* I can focus on my task without feeling overwhelmed.
* I feel positive and enthusiastic about what I am doing.
* I enjoy solving challenging problems.

## 2. Understanding Energy Sources

### Physical Energy Sources

* Getting enough physical rest and sleep.
* Getting sufficient sunlight.
* Staying hydrated.
* Eating water-rich foods such as fruits and vegetables.
* Spending some time outdoors.

### Emotional Energy Sources

* Spending time on hobbies.
* Having meaningful conversations.
* Spending time with family and friends.
* Laughing and enjoying positive moments.

### Spiritual Energy Sources

* Helping others.
* Practicing meditation.
* Spending some quiet time doing nothing.

### Intellectual Energy Sources

* Learning something new.
* Challenging myself with difficult tasks.
* Journaling and reflecting on my experiences.
* Understanding my past experiences and learning from them.

### Long-Term Practical Steps

* Completing tasks even when they feel stressful.
* Maintaining a good sleep routine.
* Getting sufficient physical movement.
* Doing light exercise.
* Practicing meditation.
* Following a structured daily routine.
* Practicing yoga and pranayama.
* Playing sports.
* Spending time on leisure activities.
* Maintaining strong relationships.
* Helping others.
* Developing self-awareness and gaining insights from my experiences.

### Short-Term Practical Steps

* Practicing guided imagery.
* Practicing progressive muscle relaxation.
* Doing breathing exercises.
* Taking a walk.
* Using the 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 counting technique when I feel angry or stressed.

### Fast-Acting Activities

* Getting a hug from a loved one.
* Using aromatherapy.
* Taking a bath.
* Giving myself a massage.
* Spending time on a hobby or passion.

## 4. Sleep is Your Superpower

### Question 4: Paraphrase the "Sleep is Your Superpower" video

* Sleep is a biological necessity, not a luxury.
* Good sleep is important for learning and memory.
* Sleep before learning prepares the brain to absorb new information.
* Lack of sleep reduces the brain's ability to create new memories.
* Sleep helps the immune system function properly.
* Lack of sleep can negatively affect cardiovascular health.
* Sleep deprivation can affect hormones and reproductive health.
* Sleep loss can increase inflammation and stress in the body.
* Good sleep allows the brain and body to recover.
* Maintaining a regular sleep schedule can improve sleep quality.
* A cool, dark and quiet bedroom can support better sleep.
* Avoiding caffeine and alcohol at inappropriate times can improve sleep.
* Sleep should be treated as an essential part of a healthy lifestyle.

### Question 5: What are some ideas that you can implement to sleep better?

* Maintain a fixed sleep and wake-up time every day.
* Aim for around 7–9 hours of sleep.
* Avoid using my phone or laptop immediately before sleeping.
* Avoid caffeine late in the day.
* Keep my bedroom cool, dark and quiet.
* Avoid heavy meals close to bedtime.
* Avoid unnecessary daytime naps if they affect my night sleep.
* Follow a relaxing bedtime routine.
* Exercise regularly during the day.
* Avoid working or studying in bed.
* Give myself enough time to relax before going to sleep.
